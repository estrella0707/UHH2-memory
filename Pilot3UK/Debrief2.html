We want to make it clear that it is NOT the intention of researchers to establish a causal link between immigration and crime rates.

Evidence-based research consistently shows no simple or direct link between immigration and increased crime. While certain groups and contexts may see small rises in property crime, immigrants are not more likely to commit violent crimes and often have lower overall crime rates than native-born populations. Economic opportunities, community attitudes, and integration policies play significant roles in shaping these outcomes, and public perceptions often do not align with empirical evidence.

Reference:
1. Bianchi, M., Buonanno, P., & Pinotti, P. (2012). Do Immigrants Cause Crime. Journal of the European Economic Association, 10, 1318-1347.
2. Bell, B., Fasani, F., & Machin, S. (2013). Crime and Immigration: Evidence from Large Immigrant Waves. Review of Economics and Statistics, 95, 1278-1290.
3. Light, M., & Miller, T. (2018). Does undocumented immigration increase violent crime?. Criminology : an interdisciplinary journal, 56 2, 370-401.
4. Ousey, G., & Kubrin, C. (2018). Immigration and Crime: Assessing a Contentious Issue, 1, 63-84.
5. Marie, O., & Pinotti, P. (2024). Immigration and Crime: An International Perspective. Journal of Economic Perspectives.
6. Amuedo‐Dorantes, C., Bansak, C., & Pozo, S. (2020). Refugee Admissions and Public Safety: Are Refugee Settlement Areas More Prone to Crime?. International Migration Review, 55, 135-165.
