Research has shown that having stable jobs is linked to a lower risk of committing crimes. However, economic conditions independently influence both immigrant employment and overall crime rates. Economic downturns reduce job opportunities. This makes it harder for immigrants with little education to find stable jobs. At the same time, rising financial stress in the broader population leads to higher crime rates. Nonprofit employment programs provide job training, language instruction, and job placement services for immigrants. Hence, they help immigrants develop skills and secure jobs that allow them to support themselves and their families. Therefore, sufficient support for such a nonprofit employment program reduces unemployment among immigrants.
