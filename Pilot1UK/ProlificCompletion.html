While the story assumed certain potential links between employment, crime, and immigration, real-world research provides a broader and more evidence-based perspective. Here is what studies actually say about this topic. You can click the button to read corresponding research results.

Unemployment & Crime

Research consistently demonstrates a positive association between higher unemployment rates and increases in property crimes, such as burglary and theft.

1. Lin, M. (2008). Does Unemployment Increase Crime?: Evidence from U.S. Data 1974-2000. Journal of Human Resources, 43, 413-436.
2. Raphael, S., & Winter‐Ebmer, R. (2001). Identifying the Effect of Unemployment on Crime.The Journal of Law and Economics, 44, 259-283.
3. Chiricos, T. (1987). Rates of Crime and Unemployment: An Analysis of Aggregate Research Evidence. Social Problems, 34, 187-212.
4. Öster, A., & Agell, J. (2007). Crime and unemployment in turbulent times. Journal of the European Economic Association, 5, 752-775.


Employment & Crime

Research consistently explores whether employment reduces crime. The evidence shows that jobs can reduce certain types of crime, especially among high-risk groups (men and youth), but the effect depends on job quality, commitment, and the type of crime.

1. Verbruggen, J., Blokland, A., & Geest, V. (2012). Effects of Employment and Unemployment on Serious Offending in a High-Risk Sample of Men and Women from Ages 18 to 32 in the Netherlands. British Journal of Criminology, 52, 845-869.
2. Apel, R., & Horney, J. (2017). How and why does work matter? Employment conditions, routine activities, and crime among adult male offenders. Criminology, 55, 307-343.
3. Heller, S. (2014). Summer jobs reduce violence among disadvantaged youth. Science, 346, 1219-1223.
4. Mesters, G., Van Der Geest, V., & Bijleveld, C. (2015). Crime, Employment and Social Welfare: An Individual-Level Study on Disadvantaged Males. Journal of Quantitative Criminology, 32, 159-190.
5. Uggen, C. (1999). Ex-Offenders and the Conformist Alternative: A Job Quality Model of Work and Crime. Social Problems, 46, 127-151.
6. Crutchfield, R., & Pitchford, S. (1997). Work and Crime: The Effects of Labor Stratification. Social Forces, 76, 93-118.


Immigrants & Crime

Research consistently shows no simple or direct link between immigration and increased crime. While certain groups and contexts may see small rises in property crime, immigrants are not more likely to commit violent crimes and often have lower overall crime rates than native-born populations. Economic opportunities, community attitudes, and integration policies play significant roles in shaping these outcomes, and public perceptions often do not align with empirical evidence.

1. Bianchi, M., Buonanno, P., & Pinotti, P. (2012). Do Immigrants Cause Crime. Journal of the European Economic Association, 10, 1318-1347.
2. Bell, B., Fasani, F., & Machin, S. (2013). Crime and Immigration: Evidence from Large Immigrant Waves. Review of Economics and Statistics, 95, 1278-1290.
3. Light, M., & Miller, T. (2018). Does undocumented immigration increase violent crime?. Criminology : an interdisciplinary journal, 56 2, 370-401.
4. Ousey, G., & Kubrin, C. (2018). Immigration and Crime: Assessing a Contentious Issue, 1, 63-84.
5. Marie, O., & Pinotti, P. (2024). Immigration and Crime: An International Perspective. Journal of Economic Perspectives.
6. Amuedo‐Dorantes, C., Bansak, C., & Pozo, S. (2020). Refugee Admissions and Public Safety: Are Refugee Settlement Areas More Prone to Crime?. International Migration Review, 55, 135-165.
